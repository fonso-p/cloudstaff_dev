Grade by Apollo list

leads
Tiers are ranks within each Apollo list (separate campaigns): the top 10% of a list are Tier 1, the next 30% Tier 2. Tier 3 = no scored signal (a scored event (news, CQC, contract or parent), roles we fill posted in the last 30 days, or a confirmed competitor client; fit alone doesn't count), whatever the score. Equal scores share the higher tier, so a tier can be larger than its share. Leads outside the list's target industry are tiered too; the company panel says to check that the industry matches. Excluded = in administration, liquidation or strike-off.

News: what the search found

companies, by list
"Found" = at least one article confirmed to be about the company (including its own news page). "Checked, nothing found" is a real zero. "Not checked" means the search failed or didn't run, so it counts as unknown.

News events found

strongest event per company
One event per company: the high-confidence event first, then the strongest by type × recency. Medium and low confidence count at half strength in the score.

Companies House and the Gazette

companies, by list
Matched by exact legal name in the November Companies House file. "Not found" is mostly a trading name that differs from the legal name, not a missing company.

Careers pages and job feeds

companies, by list
Only "ok" gives a count of open roles. "No roles found" often means the page loads its list with JavaScript or links to a job board, so it is unknown, not zero.

Open roles, in bands

companies whose careers page was read, by list
Roles open on the company's own careers page or job system on the day of the run. Job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn, NHS Jobs) are not read, so a company can be hiring and still show "no open roles" here.

How recent the hiring is

companies with open roles, by newest posting
From each role's posted date. The score counts roles posted in the last 30 days (20+ = full hiring points); older open roles show as a reason but earn no points.

Who owns the leads

companies, by list
Companies House register of people with significant control, followed up the chain of owning companies. Owner-managed = individuals at the top, directly or through a holding company with at most 5 companies: the BDM target. "Unknown" = no company number found, or the register names no one.

Where the company number came from

companies, by list
The ownership check needs the company's registration number: the Companies House name match first, then the CQC register's provider numbers, then the number printed on the company's own website (checked against the register).

Apollo funding against the news

companies with an Apollo label, by type
Apollo gives the type of the latest funding but no date or amount. "Matches the news" = the news found the same kind of event (e.g. Apollo M&A, news: bought by…). "News shows a different event" = both have something, but not the same (e.g. Apollo Debt Financing, news: PE buyout). "Not in the news" = nothing to date it. Apollo funding earns no points either way; how to reconcile the two is still open.

Fit profile used, by industry and size

Client Propensity · industry × company size
The chance that a new client like this reaches 5+ seats in a year, 10+ in two years, or adds a second department in a year, from past Cloudstaff clients in the same industry (all countries). The seat scores also use company size: past clients of the same employee band (their researched headcount today, minus their Cloudstaff seats) against the lead\'s Apollo headcount. Size made no difference to the second-department score, so that one is by industry only. Bands are relative: High = top third of past new clients.

Market trend (context only)

News Scraper segment mode · not in the score
How UK news for each sector leans, from News Scraper segment mode: 0.5 is neutral, above means more growth, labour-shortage and offshoring stories. Context only, not in the score: tiers rank within each list, where every lead shares the same trend, so it could not change anyone\'s rank.

Leads by industry

Apollo industry → Cloudstaff industry group
Target industry is decided by activity codes (Apollo US SIC / NAICS and the Companies House UK SIC), with Apollo's one-word industry only as a fallback: e.g. Coxbench Hall Residential Care Home is "individual & family services" in Apollo but SIC 8052 (nursing care). Companies whose codes show another trade (furniture, building, staffing, design) are outside the target: they are tiered, and the company panel says to check that the industry matches.

Companies in difficulty now

From each company's status in the Companies House file. Insolvency = liquidation, administration, a company voluntary arrangement (CVA) or a receiver. Strike-off proposed is often a small company closing down, not a failure.

Most active buyers

"Controls now" counts the companies they control today, in this industry and in all. A buyer can be an investor's acquisition vehicle (Bidco, Topco) or a trading group.
