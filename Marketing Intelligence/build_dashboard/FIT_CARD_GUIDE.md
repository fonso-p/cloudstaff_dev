# Reading fit chips and fit cards

The Account Priority Dashboard marks some accounts with a green **fit chip**, such as *Likely 2nd department · 1.6×*.
A fit chip means: **accounts like this one have usually grown in this way.** It comes from five years of our own client
history, not from a guess about this particular company.

Hover over a chip (or select it with the keyboard) to open its **card**, which explains the chip.

## When a chip appears

A chip appears only when both are true:

1. **High fit:** the account is in the top third of comparable accounts for that outcome.
2. **Well above normal:** its chance is at least **1.5 times** the normal rate for that outcome.

The second rule matters because "top third" alone can still be ordinary. For example, most small clients almost never
grow, so the top third of 1–4 seat clients starts at a 5% chance, which is *below* the usual 7%. Requiring 1.5× means
every chip marks a real step above the norm. Chips are meant to be **rare**.

## The chips, by list

### Organic Growth (existing clients)

| Chip | Who can get it | What it predicts, within 12 months | Compared with | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Likely 5+ seats** | Clients with 1–4 seats | Grows to 5 or more seats | Past clients with 1–4 seats (usual rate 7%) | **Strong** |
| **Likely 10+ seats** | Clients with 5–9 seats | Grows to 10 or more seats | Past clients with 5–9 seats (usual 18%) | Good |
| **Likely 20+ seats** | Clients with 10–19 seats | Grows to 20 or more seats | Past clients with 10–19 seats (usual 22%) | Good |
| **Likely 2nd department** | Clients with **one** department | Adds a second department (after its launch period) | Past one-department clients (usual 12%) | Moderate |

- A client gets at most **one seat chip**: the one for its next step up. Clients with 20+ seats get no seat chip.
- **Department chips are only for clients with one department.** The aim is to find clients that start with one
  department and grow past it. Clients that already have several departments get no department chip.
- **The launch period doesn't count.** New clients often bring their planned team on in stages over the first few months.
  So a department first staffed in a client's **first 6 months** counts as part of its launch, not as a second
  department. A *Likely 2nd department* chip on a new client means "likely to add a department after the launch".
- Today: 115 *Likely 5+ seats*, 22 *10+*, 7 *20+* and 84 *2nd department*, out of 1,110 clients.

### New Logo (prospects)

| Chip | Who can get it | What it predicts, if we sign them | Compared with | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Likely 5+ seats** | All prospects | Grows to 5 or more seats within 1 year | **The average prospect on the list** (about 6%) | Weak: segment level |
| **Likely 10+ seats** | All prospects | Grows to 10 or more seats within 2 years | Past new clients (usual 5%) | Weak: segment level |
| **Likely 2nd department** | All prospects | Adds a second department within 1 year | Past new clients (usual 27%) | Weak: segment level |

- **Prospects are scored on industry and country only.** Before a company signs we don't know its first role or how many
  staff it will start with. So every prospect with the same industry and country gets the same answer: **the chip
  describes the segment, not the company.**
- **Why "Likely 5+ seats" compares with other prospects:** because we know so little about a prospect, prospects score
  lower than past clients did (about 6% against 10%). Comparing them with past clients, no prospect would ever get the
  chip. Comparing with the other prospects on the list is a fair, like-for-like comparison. The card says which
  comparison it uses.
- A prospect shows at most one seat chip (*10+* if it qualifies, otherwise *5+*).
- The 2nd department chip can't say *which* department, since that depends on the role they start with.
- Today: 20 *Likely 5+ seats* (Technology in the US; Hospitality, Travel & Leisure in Australia and the US; Education &
  Training in Australia; Financial Services in the US), no *10+*, and 8 *2nd department*, out of 217 prospects.

### By role

Each role sees the chips on its own lists. BDM and Marketing have both an Organic Growth and a New Logo list. SDR has
New Logo only, so SDRs see only prospect chips.

## Reading the card

### Example: Organic Growth, *Likely 2nd department · 1.8×*

| On the card | What it means |
|---|---|
| **22%** chance of adding a second department within 12 months | Of every 100 past clients that looked like this one (size, time with us, industry, country, departments so far), about 22 added a second department within the next year. The estimates have been checked against history: accounts given about 20% did add one about 20% of the time. |
| **1.8×** the usual rate (12%, the line on the bar) | A typical one-department client has a 12% chance; this one is 1.8 times as likely. **This multiple is the number to focus on.** It is also the pill on the chip. |
| **High fit** | Top third of comparable past clients for this outcome. |
| **Why:** started in: Administrative | What lifts this account's chance most compared with a typical client: industry, country or the department they started in. Size and recent growth, which you can already see on the row, are summarised as "Mostly its size and recent growth" rather than repeated as numbers. Time with us is never given as a reason (it describes a stage, not the client); when it is the only driver, the card says "In line with clients at this stage". This is a pattern seen in past clients, not a cause. |
| **Departments now:** Accounting | What the client already has with us. |
| **Often added next:** Administrative 13%, BPO Voice 10% | Among similar clients that added a department, the most common next ones. No single department dominates, so use these as **conversation starters**, not forecasts. |
| **Unusually likely:** Human Resources 1.3× (3%) | Departments this kind of client adds more often than average. A niche angle worth a question. |
| *Top third of past one-department clients* | The group this account was compared with. |
| *Departments first staffed in a client's first 6 months count as its launch team* | So the chance is about a real second department, not the rest of the original team arriving. |

### Example: New Logo, *Likely 5+ seats · 1.7×*

| On the card | What it means |
|---|---|
| **10%** chance of reaching 5+ seats within 1 year of signing | Past clients with this industry and country profile grew to 5+ seats in their first year about 10% of the time. |
| **1.7×** the average prospect (6%) | This profile is 1.7 times as likely as the average prospect on the list. |
| **Why:** industry: Technology; country: United States | What lifts the profile above the average. |
| **Profile:** Technology · United States | The segment the score applies to. Every prospect in it gets the same score. |
| **Past new clients:** 10% reached 5+ seats in their first year | Context: the rate for clients that did sign, when their first role and team size were known. |
| *Compared with: the other prospects on the list* | The comparison this chip uses. |

### Seat chips on Organic Growth

They read the same way. For example, *Likely 10+ seats · 2.0×* with a **35%** chance means that 35 of every 100 similar
clients with 5–9 seats grew to 10 or more within a year, about twice the usual 18%.

## What "reaching" a seat level means

A client counts as having **reached** 5, 10 or 20 seats only if it **stays at that level for at least a month**. Short
spikes don't count, such as:

- a replacement starting before the person they replace has left, so headcount briefly reads one higher
- a short trial or a one-week overlap

So "grew to 10 seats" means **real, lasting growth**, not a blip.

## How fit affects the priority score

Fit is part of the priority score, alongside the timing signals (website activity, news and jobs, seat growth, flags):

| | Organic Growth | New Logo |
|---|---|---|
| Fit points (of 100) | **25** | **20** |
| Made up of | Next seat step 15 points, second department 10 | Seat growth (5+ or 10+, whichever is better) 15 points, second department 5 |
| Each part | High fit = full, Medium = half, Low = none | Same |

- Clients with **several departments score 0 on the department part**, so their fit is at most 15 of 25 points.
  One-department clients that are likely to add a second department rank above them.
- Accounts without a fit score (for example, clients with no current seats) get the average fit for their list, and a
  yellow *No fit data* chip.
- The points use the High / Medium / Low bands. The chips use the stricter 1.5× rule, so an account can earn fit points
  without showing a chip.

## Keep in mind

- **A pattern, not a promise.** Fit says what similar clients did over the last five years. It doesn't say what this
  client will do.
- **Fit is about potential; triggers are about timing.** A fit chip says the account is the kind that grows. A trigger
  event (a form fill, funding news and so on) says now is a good moment.
- **No chip doesn't mean no potential.** It means the account isn't clearly above the norm, or we lack the data to say.
